## Spain's route to the trophy

Spain entered the knockout phase against Austria, winning 3-0. It then eliminated Portugal 1-0, Belgium 2-1, and France 2-0 before meeting Argentina in the final. The title match stayed scoreless through normal time. FIFA's match report records Ferran Torres scoring the winner in the 106th minute.

The final result made Spain world champion for the second time, following its 2010 title. Argentina finished runner-up after entering the tournament as defending champion.

## How the expanded bracket worked

The 48-team field produced 12 groups. The top two teams from each group advanced, joined by the eight best third-place teams, creating a 32-team knockout field. From that point, every match was single elimination. Level matches proceeded to extra time and, if still tied, penalties.

This structure added one knockout round compared with the previous 32-team format. A finalist could therefore play eight matches across the tournament rather than seven.

## How to read penalty and extra-time results

“AET” means the winning score was settled after extra time. A penalty shootout does not change the match score for statistical purposes; the shootout result is listed separately. For example, Switzerland and Colombia drew 0-0, with Switzerland advancing 4-3 on penalties.

The third-place result, England 6-4 France, was a regulation match score according to FIFA's official bracket page, not a shootout notation.
